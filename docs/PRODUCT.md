# ScopeDelta Product Definition

## Mission

Help small software agencies and freelancers prevent unbilled scope creep by converting ambiguous client requests into reviewable, client-approved change orders.

## Initial ICP

Small software agencies and experienced freelancers that:

- deliver fixed-price or milestone-based software projects;
- work from proposals, statements of work, or agreed requirement lists;
- receive ongoing client requests through email, chat, meetings, or documents;
- lose time or margin deciding whether requests are inside the agreed scope;
- need a professional way to price and approve additional work.

Initial buyer: agency founder, delivery/project manager, technical lead, or senior freelancer.

## Core problem

After a project begins, clients request additions or modifications. Teams often perform small additions without formally comparing them with the agreed scope, quantifying impact, or obtaining commercial approval. This creates margin leakage and client disputes.

## MVP promise

> Turn a new client request into a reviewable, billable change order in minutes while keeping a human in control of the commercial decision.

## MVP workflow

1. Agency creates a client/project.
2. Agency provides the agreed project scope/SOW.
3. ScopeDelta structures the relevant deliverables, exclusions, constraints, and assumptions.
4. Agency pastes a new client request.
5. ScopeDelta compares the request with the agreed scope and proposes a classification:
   - in scope;
   - out of scope;
   - partially in scope;
   - insufficient information.
6. ScopeDelta explains the reasoning and affected scope items.
7. Agency reviews/edits the result and commercial impact.
8. ScopeDelta generates a client-facing change order.
9. Client can approve, reject, or request clarification.
10. Approval history is preserved.

## Critical product principle

AI assists analysis; it does not autonomously make contractual or commercial commitments. The agency user must be able to review and edit conclusions before a client sees them.

## Month-one success criteria

Primary business outcome: obtain paying pilot customers, not maximize feature completion.

Targets:

- at least 5 qualified agency/freelancer customer conversations;
- at least 2 real pilot users or strong pilot commitments;
- target 1 paid pilot as early as possible;
- demonstrate the complete scope → request → analysis → human review → change order → approval workflow;
- capture evidence of time saved, avoided unbilled work, or recovered revenue.

## MVP non-goals

Do not build in the initial MVP unless validated by customer evidence:

- full project management;
- team chat;
- time tracking;
- CRM;
- accounting/invoicing platform;
- native iOS/Android applications;
- automatic Gmail/Slack/WhatsApp ingestion;
- simultaneous Jira/Linear/Slack/Notion integrations;
- autonomous pricing commitments;
- enterprise workflow configuration;
- elaborate agent frameworks or speculative microservices.
