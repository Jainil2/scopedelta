# Outreach 001 — Paid-Pilot Validation

## Objective

Book qualified conversations with the first India prospect cohort and convert concrete scope-change pain into one of the EXP-001 validation signals: paid pilot, concrete pilot commitment, or willingness to test a real anonymized case.

Do not optimize for mass sending. Personalization and learning quality matter more than volume in this first cohort.

## Channel order

Use whichever founder-controlled channel is easiest to send from:

1. warm introduction, if one exists;
2. LinkedIn message to a founder/delivery/project leader;
3. company sales/contact email;
4. company contact form.

Do not purchase lead databases or paid outreach tools for this experiment.

## Initial message pattern

Keep the first message short. Do not pitch a large SaaS platform or lead with AI.

> Hi — I’m working on ScopeDelta for software agencies that lose margin when a client request lands after the scope is agreed but before anyone has clearly decided whether it is billable.
>
> I’m trying to understand how agencies handle this in practice. Could I ask you about the last request that was not obviously inside the original scope? I’m looking for a 15-minute conversation, not a generic product demo.

When useful, add one personalized sentence before the problem question.

## Personalized openings — first five

### 1. effy

Public fit signal: custom/end-to-end software delivery; one public client story specifically acknowledges scope creep as a recurring development-partnership issue.

Opening:

> I noticed one of effy’s published client stories explicitly talks about scope creep being an issue in development partnerships, which is unusually close to the problem I’m researching.

Then use the initial message pattern.

### 2. Qualhon Informatics

Public fit signal: custom platforms, SaaS/web/mobile systems, complex client workflows.

Opening:

> Qualhon’s work spans fairly complex custom platforms and operational systems, so I’m interested in what happens commercially when a client adds something after the original scope is already agreed.

Then ask about the most recent ambiguous request.

### 3. Facile Technolab

Public fit signal: custom software, SaaS/product development, modernization, remote product teams.

Opening:

> Facile positions around custom and product-development engagements where requirements can evolve over weeks or months, so I’m researching how agencies protect margin when those changes arrive mid-project.

Then ask how the last change was classified and approved.

### 4. Quixta

Public fit signal: web application/client delivery and quote/discovery workflow.

Opening:

> Quixta’s work includes custom web applications, and I’m researching the gap between the original quote/spec and the new requests that inevitably show up after delivery starts.

Then ask who decides whether those requests are billable.

### 5. Merkat Intellekt Technologies

Public fit signal: product engineering and a public 60-day MVP engagement model emphasizing predictability.

Opening:

> Your 60-day MVP model made me curious about how you preserve timeline and commercial predictability when a client asks for additional features halfway through the engagement.

Then ask for a recent real example.

## If they reply positively

Do not immediately sell. Book the conversation and ask them to bring one recent scope-change example they can discuss without exposing confidential client material.

Suggested reply:

> Great. The useful part for me is a recent concrete example rather than general process. If possible, come with one request where the team had to decide whether it was included, extra, or unclear. No client-confidential documents are needed for the call.

## Interview flow

Use `docs/experiments/EXP-001-paid-pilot-validation.md` as the full guide. The minimum sequence is:

1. What was the last request that was not clearly in the agreed scope?
2. What did the team do next?
3. Who decided whether it was billable?
4. Did engineering start before commercial approval?
5. How much time, margin, delay, or client friction did it create?
6. How did you communicate the change back to the client?
7. What part of that process is still manual or unreliable?
8. Would you test ScopeDelta on one anonymized real case?

Avoid asking "Would you use this?" as the main evidence. Concrete behavior and commitments matter more.

## Pilot transition

Offer the paid pilot only after the prospect has described a real pain that ScopeDelta addresses.

Suggested transition:

> That is exactly the workflow we’re testing. For the first three agencies, the pilot is ₹4,999 one-time for one live project, up to 30 days. We’ll take an anonymized agreed scope plus a new request, produce a cited scope comparison and change-order draft, and work with you on the output. There’s no subscription commitment. Would using the case you just described be worth testing?

Do not discount the first offer during the same conversation simply because the prospect hesitates. Record the objection first. Pricing changes require founder approval because this experiment is testing willingness to pay.

## If they say "too early / product not built"

> That’s fair. The pilot is intentionally guided rather than fully automated. What I’m trying to prove first is whether the output is commercially useful on a real case before we invest in automating the whole workflow.

## If they say "we already manage this in Jira/Excel/email"

> That may be the right answer. I’m specifically trying to understand whether the hard part is tracking the request, or comparing it to the signed scope and turning it into a clear commercial decision. How does that second part work today?

## If they say "our clients hate change orders"

> That’s useful. What do you normally do instead when the work is genuinely additional—absorb it, renegotiate informally, defer it, or change the timeline?

The goal is to learn whether the product should improve the commercial conversation, not force a formal process customers will reject.

## Daily first-cohort target

For the initial experiment:

- send 5 high-quality personalized messages;
- follow up once after 2–3 business days if there is no response;
- do not send repeated follow-ups after that during the first experiment;
- record sent/replied/booked/interviewed/pilot outcomes without storing unnecessary personal data.

## Evidence recording

After every meaningful reply or conversation, update `docs/research/customer-discovery.md` with:

- prospect type and role category;
- pricing model if learned;
- concrete recent scope-change example;
- current process;
- business impact;
- willingness to test anonymized material;
- willingness to pay / exact objection;
- repeated language or patterns.

Do not treat a polite positive reply as validation. Payment, real-case participation, concrete commitments, and repeated pain are the meaningful signals.
